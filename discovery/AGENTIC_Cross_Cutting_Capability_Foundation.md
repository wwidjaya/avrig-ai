## Purpose

The cross-cutting capability foundation provides the common control fabric for AGENTIC across the five generations of Agentic Engineering. These capabilities apply across lifecycle stages; they are not additional lifecycle stages.

## Cross-cutting principles and boundaries

### Human decision authority

The principle for Human Decision & Approval is:

> **Agent recommends. Human decides.**

An Agent may analyze, recommend, explain, compare alternatives, identify risks and assumptions, provide evidence, and propose changes. An Agent-generated recommendation does not become approved or authoritative without an explicit human decision being recorded.

### Lifecycle orchestration and autonomy are distinct

**Lifecycle Orchestration** determines *what happens next*.

**Autonomy & Control** determines *how much an Agent is allowed to decide and act without human intervention*.

The core autonomy question is:

> **What level of autonomy may an Agent exercise, within what boundaries, and when must control return to a human?**

The core principle is:

> **Autonomy is granted explicitly, constrained by policy and context, observable during execution, and progressively earned through validated performance.**

Autonomy and control include policy, authority, levels, action boundaries, human intervention, delegation, supervision and monitoring, guardrail enforcement, escalation and de-escalation, action validation, performance and trust, and related control responsibilities. Autonomy does not remove governance, human decision authority, or applicable approval requirements.

### Shared control fabric

The ten domains are cross-cutting capabilities intended to support the five generations of Agentic Engineering. They establish shared knowledge, meaning, decisions, traceability, impact awareness, collaboration, governance, agent context and reasoning, lifecycle sequencing, and bounded autonomy.

## Conclusion and next design step

The cross-cutting capability foundation is complete. The next logical step is **not another cross-cutting capability**. Validate these ten capabilities against the **5-generation Agentic Engineering maturity model** before proceeding into detailed lifecycle capabilities.

That validation should answer:

> **Which capabilities are required at each generation, and how does AGENTIC evolve from Agent-assisted engineering to genuinely autonomous engineering?**

The recommended starting point is **Generation 1: AI-Assisted Engineering**, proceeding one generation at a time.
